## Why the coupon-code hunt keeps coming up short

Target does issue public promo codes, but they're rarely the big ones. The headline savings are structured as *offers* — either clipped in the app or applied automatically at checkout — not as codes you find and type in.

The practical takeaway: treat third-party coupon pages as a hint, and treat the Target app as the source of truth.

## The rule that decides everything: three coupons per item

Before you clip anything, understand that Target doesn't allow unlimited stacking. It allows stacking of *different kinds* of discounts, up to three on a single item:

- one **manufacturer coupon** (paper or digital)
- one **Target Circle offer** for a broad category
- one **Target Circle offer** for that specific item

You cannot stack two manufacturer coupons on the same product. That's the wall most people hit. If you've got a paper Pampers coupon and a digital Pampers coupon, you're picking one.

The app labels which type you're looking at, which saves a lot of guesswork. Also worth knowing: Target doesn't accept expired coupons, so a drawer full of 2023 inserts is decoration.

## Timing beats cleverness

Three timing facts matter more than any coupon-hunting technique.

Circle offers refresh Sunday between midnight and 2 a.m. Central. If Friday's deals look thin, wait.

Target runs seasonal sales — Black Friday and Cyber Monday, Target Circle Week events that hit up to 50% off across categories, and category-specific promotions like the back-to-school period where pens, notebooks and backpacks drop heavily. Gift card promos cluster around these windows.

Price adjustments exist. You have 14 days from purchase with proof of receipt to request a match, and Target matches competitors plus its own later price drops. Then there's the obvious one: free standard shipping kicks in at $35, or with a Circle Card regardless of total.
